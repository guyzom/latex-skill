\begin{abstract}
A one-dimensional quadratic gives a transparent example of regularization.
We derive its unique minimizer, describe the effect of a nonnegative penalty,
and compare three illustrative parameter choices. The example is mathematical
exposition, not an empirical study: every value in the table and figure follows
from the stated model.
\end{abstract}
\section{Model and assumptions}
Let $a$ be a fixed real target and let $x$ be a real decision variable. A
nonnegative parameter $\lambda$ weights a quadratic penalty. The objective is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:objective}
Q_\lambda(x)=(x-a)^2+\lambda x^2,\qquad \lambda\geq 0.
\end{equation}
The first term measures deviation from the target; the second penalizes the
size of the decision. Equation~\eqref{eq:objective} defines the model used
throughout this note. There are no units or observational errors in this
dimensionless example. For general background on convex optimization, see
Boyd and Vandenberghe~\cite{boyd2004}.
\section{Solution and interpretation}
Differentiating and collecting terms gives
% This intermediate working uses align*; only central or cited formulas get tags.
\begin{align*}
Q'_\lambda(x)&=2(x-a)+2\lambda x\\
             &=2(1+\lambda)x-2a.
\end{align*}
The stationary point is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solution}
x_\lambda^\star=\frac{a}{1+\lambda}.
\end{equation}
\begin{paperlemma}\label{lem:minimum}
For every real $a$ and every $\lambda\geq 0$, the point in
\eqref{eq:solution} is the unique global minimizer of
\eqref{eq:objective}.
\end{paperlemma}
\begin{proof}
Completing the square yields
\[
Q_\lambda(x)=(1+\lambda)
 \left(x-\frac{a}{1+\lambda}\right)^2+
 \frac{\lambda a^2}{1+\lambda}.
\]
The squared term is nonnegative and its coefficient is positive. It vanishes
only at the point in \eqref{eq:solution}, proving existence and uniqueness.
\end{proof}
The minimum objective value is a second useful result of the model:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimum-value}
Q_\lambda(x_\lambda^\star)=\frac{\lambda a^2}{1+\lambda}.
\end{equation}
\noeqref{eq:minimum-value}
When $\lambda=0$, the minimizer equals $a$. Increasing the penalty moves the
minimizer toward zero. This is an algebraic consequence of the chosen objective,
rather than evidence that any particular penalty is suitable for measured data.
\section{Illustrative values}
Take $a=2$. The entries in \autoref{tab:penalties} are obtained by substituting
each parameter into \eqref{eq:solution} and then evaluating
\eqref{eq:objective} at the minimizer.

\section{Scope}
This calculation establishes an exact result for one specified quadratic.
Applying regularization to a larger problem also requires a justified model,
appropriate units, and a defensible choice of penalty. Those decisions do not
follow from this illustrative calculation.
